THE DECISION
  • The bet: A buyer at $208.67 is betting revenue can meet or beat the $8.15 billion 2026 guide and that earnings rise fast enough to support about 89 times forward earnings (price divided by expected profit).
  • You're wrong if: growth slows well below the latest 92.8 percent rate, a major government contract is cut, or the multiple falls sharply even while profits still rise.
  • What the price already assumes: At 81.5 times trailing sales and a 0.7 percent free cash flow yield (cash left after equipment spending, divided by market value), a great deal of good news is already priced in. The average analyst target of $205.27 is 1.6 percent below today's price. Analyst targets are opinions and are often wrong.
  • Fits you if: you can hold for several years, sit through a drop toward $165 to $188 or worse, and you do not need to trade every headline.
  • Doesn't fit you if: you need the money within a year, cannot tolerate a large drop in a stock with a beta of 1.6 (it usually moves more than the market), or you would sell a dip under the 50-day average of $180.36.
  • Case gets stronger if: the November 2, 2026 report meets or beats the $2.160 to $2.164 billion quarter guide, and U.S. commercial remaining deal value holds near or above $6.238 billion.
  • Case gets weaker if: the stock fails at the $212.94 high and closes under $187.82, or full-year revenue guidance is cut below $8.150 billion.
BEST BULL POINTS
  • Palantir is already highly profitable while sales are still growing very fast.
    Evidence: The August 3, 2026 company release reported second-quarter revenue of $1.935 billion, up 93 percent from a year earlier. GAAP operating income was $912 million, a 47 percent margin. Yahoo Finance shows an 84.8 percent gross margin and a 49.0 percent profit margin.
    Why it matters: For a hold of a year or more, real profit matters more than a growth story. This software business already turns a large share of sales into reported profit.
    Other side: That same quarter included $265 million of stock-based compensation, so part of the cost to owners is new shares rather than cash.
  • Management raised the 2026 outlook, and newly closed contract value is still large.
    Evidence: The August 3, 2026 release raised full-year revenue guidance to $8.150–$8.158 billion, against $4.5 billion of 2025 revenue in the annual filing. Adjusted free-cash-flow guidance was raised to $4.5–$4.7 billion. Second-quarter total contract value closed was $3.373 billion, up 49 percent, and U.S. commercial remaining deal value was $6.238 billion, up 124 percent.
    Why it matters: A long-term buyer needs signs that growth has contracted work behind it, not only one strong past quarter.
    Other side: Remaining performance obligations were $4.9 billion at June 30, 2026, and only about 43 percent was expected within 12 months. Deal value is not guaranteed revenue.
  • The company holds a large cash cushion and has no long-term borrowings.
    Evidence: The June 30, 2026 filing showed $2.030 billion of cash and $7.379 billion of marketable securities, and it listed no long-term borrowings. Noncurrent operating lease liabilities were $211 million. Yahoo Finance shows net cash of $9.20 billion.
    Why it matters: Net cash, meaning cash minus debt, gives the company room to keep operating if a contract slips or growth slows.
    Other side: No strong counter in the evidence. Cash does not make a price of 81.5 times sales cheap.
BEST BEAR POINTS
  • The share price already assumes years of exceptional results.
    Evidence: Yahoo Finance shows a price of $208.67, a price-to-sales ratio of 81.5, a trailing price-to-earnings ratio of 179.9, and a forward price-to-earnings ratio of 89.0. Free-cash-flow yield, which is free cash flow divided by market value, is 0.7 percent. The average analyst target is $205.27, about 1.6 percent below the price.
    Why it matters: Over a year or more, a rich multiple can fall even if the business keeps growing. A yield under 1 percent is very low.
    Other side: The lower forward price-to-earnings ratio means analysts expect earnings to rise. Guidance also implies 2026 revenue near $8.15 billion versus $4.5 billion in 2025.
  • A large share of sales depends on government contracts that can end early.
    Evidence: The 2025 annual filing said government customers supplied 54 percent of $4.5 billion in revenue. Many contracts can be terminated for convenience, and options are not guaranteed. The company excluded $12.3 billion of IDIQ awards whose funding was not set. On October 12, 2026, the BBC reported that a UK special-operations unit ended a two-year Palantir trial.
    Why it matters: A delayed or canceled public contract can hurt growth that today’s price treats as durable.
    Other side: U.S. government revenue still rose 90 percent in the second quarter, and no customer exceeded 10 percent of revenue.
  • Adjusted cash figures add back a large stock expense, and the recent run has been fast.
    Evidence: Stock-based compensation was $265 million in the second quarter, and the company adds it back in adjusted operating income and adjusted free cash flow. Trailing free cash flow was about $3.36 billion. The price is up 56.0 percent in three months and sits just under the $212.94 52-week high, with a 14-day RSI of 75.6.
    Why it matters: Owners can be diluted even when adjusted free cash flow looks strong. Buying here means paying near the top of a fast rise.
    Other side: GAAP operating margin was still 47 percent after that stock expense. Past chart levels do not decide a multi-year business result.

  • How much signed deal value will become recognized revenue. Comparing later revenue with remaining deal value and remaining performance obligations would settle that.

Management raised 2026 revenue guidance to $8.150-8.158B, against $4.5B in 2025. It also raised adjusted free cash flow guidance to $4.5-4.7B. Trailing free cash flow is already $3.36B.

Forward P/E of 89 versus trailing 179.9 implies earnings are expected to roughly double.

The risks are real. At 81.5 times sales the stock is expensive, and the average target of $205.27 sits below the price. RSI of 75.6 is overbought, so scaling in is wiser than chasing. Third-quarter results arrive November 2.

BULL REPLY (Claude)
The bear is right on valuation. 81.5 times sales is rich, RSI of 75.6 is stretched, and the $205.27 average target sits below the price. But the bear's other points are weaker.

First, the stock-based compensation point. The 47% GAAP operating margin ($912M) already absorbs the $265M quarterly expense. The add-back only affects adjusted figures, and the profit is real on a GAAP basis.

Second, the 27% receivables figure. The filing says no customer exceeded 10% of revenue, so this is a collection timing item, not revenue dependence.

Third, government exposure. Termination for convenience is standard contract language, and U.S. government revenue still grew 90% in the second quarter.

Fourth, the multiples. Trailing P/E of 179.9 uses earnings from before 93% growth. Forward P/E of 89 is about half of that, and 2026 revenue guidance of $8.15B compares with $4.5B in 2025. A 0.7% FCF yield uses trailing cash flow, while guidance is $4.5-4.7B versus $3.36B trailing.

Over a year or more, earnings growth can close the valuation gap. Scale in near the $187.82 support rather than chasing.
